The problem

Most sites were built for steady hands and perfect eyesight.

Tiny tap targets. Gray on gray. Pages with no place for your eyes to rest. Enterprise overlays that cost more than a small team’s whole tooling budget. Plenty of products ask you to change how you browse so the tool can work.

We build the other way around. Controls that sit in your Chrome browser or on the sites people already visit, so reading, clicking, and applying for work don’t require a different workflow.

1 in 4

US adults has a disability that can affect how they use the web. CDC

  • Unreadable layouts Low contrast, tight spacing, walls of text.
  • Hard-to-hit controls Buttons and links sized for ideal conditions only.
  • Locked-out tooling Enterprise widgets priced out of reach for most sites.

What we ship instead: hover-to-click and reading mode in Chrome; a 15+ control toolbar you embed with one script; a shift feed ranked by accommodation overlap.

Who it’s for

If the default UI costs you energy.

Accessibility Surfer and AccessFlow are for people managing tremors, RSI, low vision, dyslexia, eye gaze, or plain screen fatigue. Axol Work is for Prospects and Recruiters who treat accommodations as first-class data.

Motor, pain, RSI

Tremors, chronic pain, spinal cord injury, MS, ALS, a wrist in a cast. Hover-to-click means a day of YouTube, forms, and shopping without burning out your hands.

Vision, dyslexia, focus

When type fights you, Surfer and AccessFlow retune fonts, spacing, and contrast on the page you’re already on, or strip it to reading mode.

Gaze, head pointer, fatigue

If moving the cursor is easier than pressing, dwell clicking is built for that gap: eye gaze, head trackers, mouth sticks, joysticks, or late-day fatigue.

What we build

Three products. Three install paths.

Chrome Web Store for individuals. One script or npm import for site owners. A signed-in web app for Prospects and Recruiters.

  1. 01

    Accessibility Surfer

    Free Chrome extension

    Dwell clicking, font/size/spacing/contrast controls, and reading mode. Preferences stay in Chrome storage. Works on YouTube, marketplaces, and everyday sites.

  2. 02

    AccessFlow

    CDN script or npm

    Visitor-controlled toolbar. Paste https://axolassist.com/cdn/accessflow.js or run npm i @axol-assist/accessflow. No backend; settings persist per visitor.

  3. 03

    Axol Work

    Web app · live preview

    Shift marketplace ranked by accommodation fit. Prospects set needs once; Recruiters tag supports; badges like “3 of 4 needs met” appear before you open a listing.

  • Settings stored in the browser, not our servers
  • No account needed for Surfer or AccessFlow
  • Keyboard paths and :focus-visible on this site
  • WCAG-oriented contrast on marketing pages
  • Respects prefers-reduced-motion
  • Chrome MV3 extension + CDN/npm packages

Why Axol Assist exists

Meet people in the tabs they already have open.

Too many accessibility products are priced for procurement teams, buried three menus deep, or shipped as overlays site owners never turn on. People still fight 11px gray text and 24px click targets alone.

We ship Accessibility Surfer for individuals, AccessFlow for developers who can paste a script, and Axol Work for hiring where accommodations are structured fields instead of a buried “other” box.

What we won’t promise: that any toolbar replaces building accessible HTML. Surfer and AccessFlow help visitors adapt the page in front of them. Sites still owe WCAG work underneath.
